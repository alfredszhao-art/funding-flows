About Recommended Charity Fund Our Recommended Charities

The Recommended Charity Fund is disbursed twice per year (in February and August) according to the distribution that our Programs team determines to be the most impactful at that time. Because February’s disbursement falls soon after our annual release of new charity recommendations and August’s disbursement falls soon after our mid-year check on charities’ Room for More Funding, we are able to use current information to guide our allocation decisions.

January 1 – June 30, 2026

In August, we distributed $1,293,213 among our 10 Recommended Charities. This included $227,107 from our Greatest Needs Fund. Grant decisions were made based on each charity’s revenue and Room for More Funding. Additional reasoning for each charity’s grant amount is detailed below.

Recommended Charity Fund Grants: August 2026
Recommended Charity Amount Percentage Reasoning
Animal Welfare Observatory $67,818 4.97% Smaller funding gap because of a recent large influx of unexpected funding, but still have room to absorb more for follow-up on aquatic animal commitments and improving internal MEL processes
Aquatic Life Institute $76,563 5.61% • Funding will go toward reclassifying long-term contractors to employees, corporate engagement targeting suppliers, and building out a framework for evaluating wild capture fisheries
• Uncertain cost-effectiveness for corporate outreach wins because of the focus on shrimp eyestalk ablation and electrical stunning of fishes, and attribution uncertainty
Çiftlik Hayvanlarını Koruma Derneği $144,381 10.57% • Funding will go to their fish welfare research regranting program, which has strong support but uncertain absorptive capacity
• Cage-free accountability work continues to look counterfactually strong as the only player in the region
Dansk Vegetarisk Forening $161,158 11.80% • Strong track record in policy and diplomacy, headlined by making the Danish government’s plant-based fund permanent, with external collaborators confirming the value of their best-practice sharing
• Uncertain cost-effectiveness for international expansion work and some uncertainty around their recent pace of growth
Good Food Fund $144,381 10.57% • Marginal funding prioritizes the China EAT-Lancet report and nutritional guidelines involvement, with clear policy avenues and relationships built through the Good Food Summit
• Plant-forward rather than fully plant-based positioning remains a concern for small-bodied animals
Shrimp Welfare Project $93,339 6.83% Marginal funding would support evidence building rather than scaling shrimp stunning: field trials, stunner efficacy testing, MEL/verification, and industry implementation. Relatively high uncertainty regarding the evidence base
Sinergia Animal $203,455 14.90% • Marginal funding targets a retention and succession plan addressing turnover that is affecting program continuity, which leadership identifies as their top priority. It is not fully clear whether funding alone resolves this bottleneck
• Work quality remains strong
Sociedade Vegetariana Brasileira $67,818 4.97% Marginal plans are varied and overall of moderate priority, such as increasing salaries, hiring a nutritionist to engage with universities, and commissioning consumer research to support their programs
The Humane League $212,199 15.54% • Large, credible funding gap supporting time-sensitive priorities, such as campaigns against major retailers
• High confidence in the organization and their current prioritization
Wild Animal Initiative $194,711 14.26% Marginal plans appear highly effective, even alongside substantially increased funding from other sources. These include expanding internal research capacity, external research grants, and scaling up their fellowship program for candidates with long-term career potential in wild animal welfare
