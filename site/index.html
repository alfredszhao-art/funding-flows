Every grant, as a dot

One row per funder, time running left to right, each dot a single grant sized by its amount. Dots outside the chosen period fade back, so the recent money stands out against the history.

Hover a dot for the grantee and purpose; click it to open the funder’s own record. Hollow rings are regrants — money passed on to another grantmaker, shown for transparency but never added to any total. Grants known only to the month, quarter or year are placed at the start of that period.

Year by year

Disclosed grant volume by calendar year, for the causes selected above. The full history always shows here, whichever period is chosen.

Which animals

Money in the chosen period, split by the species a grant’s title or grantee mentions.

Species are read from grant text, so only grants that name one are counted, and a grant naming several counts for each. “Aquatic (general)” means fish welfare grants that do not name a species.

Biggest recipients

Who received the most in the chosen period, across the causes selected above. Several grants to one organisation add up.

Regrants are left out, and so are bundles the funder publishes as “Various individuals and organisations”. Fiscally sponsored projects appear under the name the funder published.

How much of the whole is this?

Published estimates of total movement spending, set against what this tracker can see in the same year.

Who funds what

Each funder’s volume, typical grant, recent activity and focus, drawn only from grants they have disclosed. Open a row to see what they funded lately and whether they are taking applications.

What is coming next

Forward-looking money, ordered by how actionable it is: calls you can answer today first, long-range projections last. Everything is a stated intention with a source and a confidence level — not a prediction, and not yet a grant.

Every disclosed grant

The full record behind the charts. Search by grantee or purpose; click a column to sort. Each amount links to the funder’s own page.
